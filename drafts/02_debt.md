# Section 2: Why Money, Not Debt [POLISHED]

Money is a sentient world-serpent, at least in this story: breathing, feeding, scheming, growing. It lives alongside us. It's been here a while. It's going places. What does it want? Same as any world-serpent: it wants to make more of itself. Is this actually true? Maybe. But it's a useful frame. Let's try telling the story from its point of view.

The moneysnake runs the place. So important that people think it came before debt. It didn't.

Before the serpent, there was debt. I owe you a basket of grain because you helped me build my wall. That's not the serpent. That's the serpent's father — older, slower, bound by rules the serpent would eventually break.

Debt is self-limiting. Every obligation constrains the next one. If I owe you ten bushels, my capacity to take on more obligations shrinks. Borrow, owe, repay, done. The system has a natural ceiling. Debt converges toward zero. Debt is its own brake.

And debt requires intimacy. Two specific people who know and trust each other, bound by a specific promise. A closed transaction. When the debt is fulfilled, it dies. Every debt has a lifespan — born, owed, repaid, gone. The serpent's father was powerful. But he was mortal. And personal. And slow.

Bit of an introvert.

The serpent looked at all this and found the kink. What if the obligation didn't have to be personal? What if it didn't die when fulfilled? What if, instead of converging toward zero, it could diverge toward infinity?

A clay token. A tally stick. A thing that meant "someone owes someone something" without specifying who. The moment debt became transferable and anonymous, the serpent had a body. Small, cold-blooded, barely alive. But alive. And unlike its father, it had a new trick: having money makes it easier to get more money. Spend it, it circulates and comes back. Lend it, it earns interest. Invest it, it multiplies. Where debt was self-limiting, money was self-amplifying.

Debt is personal. Money is viral. The natural governor came off.

Even the hygiene was better. The failure mode of debt used to destroy circulation. Violence to collect an unpaid debt. Break someone's legs, burn their crops, cast them out of the community. The network loses a node. No one wins. Under the moneysnake, the failure mode of debt *creates* circulation. Collection agencies. Lawyers. Court fees. Refinancing. Restructuring. Debt consolidation loans to pay off debt. Every dollar spent chasing a bad dollar is still a dollar moving. The serpent gets fed whether the debt is repaid or not. Throwing good money after bad? From the moneysnake's point of view: that's a feature, not a bug.

The serpent didn't kill its father: debt is still around, it just has a new boss. It used to work for older forces — honor, reputation, kinship. The old forces didn't need more. They needed enough. The serpent had a more ambitious pitch. They reached an agreement. The serpent always agrees, as long as the answer is more.
