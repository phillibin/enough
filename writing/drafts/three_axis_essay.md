# Rigorous Optimism About the Future of Work
# STATUS: Near-final draft from Feb 2026. May not make it into the book as-is.
# This is the first piece in the project — the three-axis framework.

We can't get to a good place if we can't articulate what a good place looks like.

The conversation about AI and the future of work is stuck. Pessimists enumerate disasters. Optimists gesture vaguely at abundance. Neither side is specific about what "good" actually means, and I think that's because "work" is a word that's doing too much heavy lifting. It means at least three different things at once, and until we pull them apart, we'll keep talking past each other.

Here are the three dimensions:

**How the tasks feel**, from play to toil. Do you like doing it, or do you endure it?

**How much they matter**, from bullshit to important. When you finish, did anything actually matter?

**How much they pay**, from unpaid to paid. Does it pay the bills or drain your savings?

These three dimensions are independent. Any combination is possible, and that's what makes them useful. They give us a coordinate system. *Bullshit unpaid toil* is the worst location; *important paid play* is the best.

For most of civilization, certainly through the early agricultural and industrial eras, the vast majority of people had one thing in their lives. That thing was *bullshit unpaid toil*. Breaking your body to survive, with no pay as we'd recognize it, no meaning beyond subsistence, and no enjoyment. One thing, and it was the worst possible combination.

Then progress happened. Industrialization, productivity gains, labor movements, the weekend, the middle class, the standing desk. Life split into two things. Think about something you spend a lot of time on. It's probably some version of *bullshit paid toil*, the thing that pays the bills but doesn't thrill you, plus some *unpaid play* on the side, some of it bullshit, some of it important. That's where most people live right now, toggling between these two objects. We even named the tension: work-life balance.

Of course this can go wrong. AI could collapse the "paid" axis for millions of people while automating the parts that already felt like play, leaving a new kind of *bullshit unpaid toil*. I won't dwell on it because it doesn't lack for spokespeople.

The hope is that AI pushes us from two things back to one. But this time, the one thing is *important paid play*. Not a return to the single-object life of survival, but a convergence toward integration. The split between "work" and "life" doesn't get better balanced. It dissolves, because it's no longer necessary.

The architect who loves designing but spends half her time on permit paperwork. The teacher who got into it for the kids but drowns in administration. Have you ever said, "I love my job, but..." If we get this right, AI is coming for that but.

This is not a prediction, and not even a plan. It's a destination. I'm trying to describe where we could get to before we start the hard work of getting there.

This is rigorous optimism. Not the belief that everything will be fine, which is naive. Not the belief that everything will be terrible, which is lazy and self-fulfilling. The belief that you have to define how things can go right, concretely and specifically, so that you can do the work of making them go that way.

So here it is: *important paid play*, for as many people as possible. That's the destination. Now let's all talk about how to get there.
