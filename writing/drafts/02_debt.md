# Section 2: Why Money, Not Debt [POLISHED]

Let's suppose that money is a sentient world-serpent: breathing, feeding, scheming, growing. It lives alongside us. It's been here a while, and it's going places. It wants what any world-serpent does: to make more of itself. Is this actually true? Maybe. Let's try telling the story from its point of view.

The moneysnake runs the place. It's so important that people think it came before debt, but it didn't. Debt was there first. I owe you a basket of grain because you helped me build my wall. That's not money, that's the serpent's father — older, slower, bound by rules the moneysnake would eventually break.

Debt is self-limiting. Every obligation constrains the next one. If I owe you ten bushels, my capacity to take on more debt shrinks. Borrow, owe, repay. The system has a natural ceiling. Debt stays finite; its own brake.

And debt requires intimacy. Two specific people who know and trust each other, bound by a promise. A closed transaction. When the debt is fulfilled, it dies. Every debt has a lifespan — born, owed, repaid, gone. The serpent's father was powerful, but he was personal and slow.

Bit of an introvert.

The serpent found the kink. What if the obligation didn't have to be personal? What if it didn't die when fulfilled? What if, instead of something to be mildly ashamed of, it was something people craved?

From obligation to desire. Instead of staying finite, it would reach toward infinity and immortality. 

The moneysnake grew from a clay token or a shell or a tally stick. A thing that meant "someone owes someone something" without specifying who, or what, just how much. The moment debt became transferable and anonymous, the serpent had a body. It was small and cold-blooded, barely alive. It aroused jealousy and made enemies quickly. Didn't seem like much at first; merely a little convenience, a place-holder. A shortcut.  But unlike its father, it had a new trick: having money makes it easier to get more money. Spend it and it goes around, lend it and it comes back bigger. Invest it, and it returns multiples. By being a neutral measure of other things' value, it became the most valuable thing of all.

Debt is personal but money is viral. The natural governor came off.

Even the hygiene was better. When debt fails, it destroys circulation. Use violence to collect an unpaid debt? Break someone's legs, burn their crops, cast them out of the community? No one wins and the circle shrinks a little. But with money, failure mode *creates* circulation: collection agencies, lawyers, fees, penalties, refinancing, restructuring, consolidation... Every dollar spent trying to fix a failure is still a dollar moving. The serpent gets fed whether your debt is repaid or not. Throwing good money after bad? From the moneysnake's point of view: that's a feature, not a bug.

The moneysnake doesn't care if you have more money. It just wants there to be more money.

The serpent didn't kill its father: debt is still around, it just has a new boss. It used to work for older forces — honor, reputation, kinship. But the older forces didn't need more, they just needed enough. The serpent had a more ambitious pitch, so they reached an agreement. 

The serpent always agrees, as long as the answer is more.
